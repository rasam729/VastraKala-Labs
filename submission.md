## Project summary

VastraKala Labs is an AI-powered digital atelier and immersive learning platform that teaches traditional Indian couture, pattern mathematics, and living textile heritage through generative sketch synthesis, CAD dieline generation, and interactive 3D digital twin fitting. It is built for fashion students, design apprentices, and craft revivalists seeking to bridge generational artisanal wisdom with modern computational precision.

## Short answers

**1. What made you choose this problem?**

Traditional Indian fashion design and textile crafts—from Kanjeevaram Korvai interlocking weaves to Katori blouse dart mathematics and Zardozi relief—embody centuries of profound geometry and cultural identity. However, modern fashion pedagogy remains disproportionately centered around Western blocks, while Indian apparel is often taught through non-interactive, passive video tutorials that fail to teach underlying mathematical ease and grainline physics. Students frequently struggle to bridge the gap between body proportions, fabric drape, and skin undertone harmony. We built VastraKala Labs to digitize and democratize this living craft heritage, combining rigorous pattern drafting mathematics with generative AI and interactive 3D visualization.

**2. Who is it for, and how would they use it in a normal week?**

VastraKala Labs is engineered for fashion design university students, couture apprentices, freelance tailors, and textile heritage enthusiasts. In a typical week, a student takes an adaptive diagnostic placement test to generate a tailored, tailoring-stitch learning roadmap containing curated studio masterclasses and museum documentation. Throughout their coursework, they use the Unified AI Studio to sketch concepts, generate photorealistic garments with Gemini Vision, test skin tone harmony against 8 canonical undertones, and reverse-engineer photos into vector CAD dielines with exact seam allowances. They then test their designs in the 3D studio on an anatomical body mannequin and consult Stitch, their resident AI atelier mentor, for real-time formula guidance and jury evaluations.

**3. If you had another month, what would you build next?**

With another month, we would implement real-time GPU-accelerated cloth simulation (Verlet integration and mass-spring physics) in WebGL to accurately simulate the differential drape fluidity of gossamer Chanderi versus heavy Mulberry raw silk. We would also build an AI-powered zero-waste marker planning algorithm that nests vector dieline pattern pieces onto variable handloom fabric widths to completely eliminate cutting scrap in production ateliers. Finally, we would expand our platform with a Direct-to-Artisan bridge, allowing verified student digital dielines to be sent directly to registered weaver cooperatives across India for authentic small-batch sample production and fair-trade attribution.
